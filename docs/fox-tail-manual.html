Fox Tail96-partial additive oscillator · Daisy Patch SM · user manual

Fox Tail is a stereo additive oscillator. Ninety-six sine partials run at once; the panel is a set of tools for choosing which of them sound, how loud, at what frequency ratio, and where in the stereo field. There is no filter and no audio input — every shape you hear was drawn, not carved.

1 · How it makes sound

Ninety-six sine oscillators run in parallel. Partial k normally sits at k × f0 — partial 1 is the fundamental, partial 2 the octave, and so on. Every control on the panel does exactly one of four things:

What it changesControls
Which partials sound, and how loudthe eight band sliders, the FADE TILT pots, SHIFT, BRIGHT/DARK, ODD ONLY
Where the partials sit in frequencyCOARSE, V/OCT, FINE, INHARM, and the shaper (WIN START, WIN SIZE, PHI/CLUSTERS, DENSITY)
Where they sit in the stereo fieldSTEREO
Nothing you can hear directlythe two audio input jacks — Fox Tail never reads them

The bank is divided into eight bands

The eight sliders to the right of INHARM each own a slice of the harmonic series. The slices are geometric — equal width in octaves, not in harmonics — so the leftmost slider owns two partials and the rightmost owns about thirty. The panel prints where each one starts: ×1 ×2 ×4 ×7 ×13 ×23 ×41 ×73.

2 · The band sliders — level and fill

Each of sliders ×1…×73 does two jobs at once, split across its travel:

  • All the way up from zero: the band's level rises.
  • Above the halfway point, additionally: the band fills in — more of its partials start sounding, one at a time, spreading outward from a seed partial.

So all eight sliders parked at half gives you a skeleton comb: exactly eight partials, one per band, at moderate level. Push a slider past half and its band thickens up into a full cluster of harmonics. The coupling is deliberate — a band with more partials in it genuinely is louder, so level and count move together and the drawn envelope stays believable at any setting.

Practical: to draw a formant-ish hump, set the sliders in an arc and leave the bands you want thin at half. To play “additive organ”, run everything at half and use SHIFT (below) to slide the eight chosen harmonics up and down the series.

Each slider also has its own CV jack in the top row. The jack is summed with the slider and clamped, so a slider parked at zero is opened by CV, and one parked at the top ignores it.

3 · FADE TILT — where each band fills from

The six pots labelled under the FADE TILT (HP-BP-LP) bracket set the fill order for bands ×4 through ×73. (The first two bands hold two partials each, so they have no pot; their two pots on the panel row are SHIFT/FINE/STEREO's neighbours — see below.)

Pot positionFill orderSounds like
Full CCWstarts at the band's highest partial and grows downwardhighpass — the band's top edge survives
Centrestarts in the middle and grows both waysbandpass
Full CWstarts at the band's lowest partial and grows upwardlowpass

Highpass sits at CCW so that building a spectral hump is one diagonal gesture: a band to the left of your peak wants to keep the partials closest to the peak — which are its high ones — so the pots rise left-to-right exactly as the sliders do.

4 · INHARM — the stiff string

Slider 1 (with its own CV jack) bends the whole series away from whole-number ratios, using the stiff-string law fk = k·f0·√(1+B·k²). Because the stretch goes as k², the low partials stay nearly pure while the top of the bank runs sharp — piano-ish at low settings, bell and gong metallic at the top. At full travel the upper partials land several times above their harmonic positions.

Why it matters: this is the one thing a subtractive voice fundamentally cannot fake. A filter can shape a harmonic spectrum; it cannot make it inharmonic.

5 · SHIFT and FINE

SHIFT — slides the drawn shape along the series

SHIFT multiplies the coordinate that both the band envelope and the fill comb are read at, ±1 octave from centre. No partial changes frequency. Only which partials are picked, and how loud they are. Turned clockwise the shape moves up the series, counter-clockwise down. Centre is neutral.

Paired with the skeleton comb (all sliders at half) this is the single most musical gesture on the panel: eight chosen harmonics scanning up and down the series over a pitch that never moves.

FINE — ±1 semitone

COARSE covers 3.3 octaves in one sweep, which is far too coarse to tune by hand. FINE adds ±1 semitone, centre = in tune, clockwise = sharp. The firmware snaps a small deadzone at noon, so 12 o'clock is exactly in tune despite pot noise.

6 · COARSE and V/OCT

COARSE sets the fundamental over 20 Hz – 200 Hz, exponentially: the knob's centre lands on the geometric mean (~63 Hz), not the arithmetic one. The range is deliberately narrow — this is the root of an additive bank, and the partials, not the root, carry the top of the range.

The V/O jack is a calibrated 1 V/octave input, summed with COARSE and FINE. On a calibrated unit tracking holds within a few cents over two octaves. The effective fundamental is clamped to 8 Hz – 6 kHz so that a railed CV input means “very high note” rather than a silent module.

Nyquist is real. At 48 kHz an f0 of 220 Hz leaves about 109 usable partials; at 880 Hz only 27. Partials fade out over the top 5% of the range rather than switching off, and a band whose partials have all run past Nyquist pulses its LED dimly rather than just going dark.

7 · BRIGHT/DARK and ODD ONLY

The tilt switch (right-hand switch)

The underlying slope of the untouched spectrum:

PositionLawCharacter
DARK (down)1/ra sawtooth's −6 dB/octave; what an acoustic source does
BRIGHT (up)1/√requal power per octave; ~3 dB/octave brighter, ≈20 dB over the bank

BRIGHT makes every band slider equally effective, which is a lovely panel property, but it leaves the top octaves loud enough that you hear individual partials beating. DARK is the safer bed for drones. The flip is level-compensated, so it changes colour and not loudness.

ODD ONLY (gate jack)

A gate high mutes the even-numbered partials, leaving 1, 3, 5, … — hollow, clarinet-ish. Unpatched reads low, which is ALL partials, so the jack costs nothing left empty. It is a digital input: gates and squares, not CV. Under ODD ONLY, SHEPARD's glide takes twice as long to come round (see below).

8 · The shaper window — WIN START and WIN SIZE

The two big knobs marked WIN START and WIN SIZE define a movable window over the bank. Partials inside the window get retuned; partials outside stay exactly where they are. This is what lets the weird stuff happen without losing the pitch — leave the fundamental outside the window and it anchors the note while everything above it goes strange.

Both knobs are exponential over 1…96 partials, so the low end of the bank — where the ear cares — gets most of the travel. Both are CV-able (START and SIZE jacks), which makes the window itself a playable target. They mean the same thing in both modes; that is what makes a screenless panel survive the mode switch.

In SHEPARD the window start and size snap to whole partials (two at a time under ODD ONLY). That is not cosmetic: it puts every partial's window weight at exactly 0 or 1, which is what makes the endless-glide wrap seamless. It also makes “exclude the fundamental” a repeatable gesture instead of a hunt. In CLUSTER the window is continuous.

9 · CLUSTER vs SHEPARD

The left-hand switch picks what happens inside the window. The two mode knobs — the panel's PHI / CLUSTERS and WIN DUCK / DENSITY — are the only two controls that change meaning.

CLUSTER (switch down)SHEPARD (switch up)

Knob 4 is centre-neutral in both modes, with a software deadzone at noon. A stock build has no mechanical detent, so trust the sound, not the pointer: at noon nothing shifts and nothing glides.

CLUSTER

The windowed partials are grouped into clusters of m partials each, and every member slides toward one end of its own cluster. Density sets how far it gets.

  • Density 0 (knob 5 CCW): nothing moves. There is a 2% deadzone at the bottom so a pot that doesn't quite reach its end can't leave a detune behind.
  • Density 0.9–1.0: the useful zone. Members land within a hair of each other and beat — thick chorus, then slow shimmer, then near-unison. Beat periods run into seconds.
  • Knob 4 CW (collapse down): the survivors land on exact multiples of m — a comb aligned to f0, so it thickens the note.
  • Knob 4 CCW (collapse up): the same spacing, offset by m−1, so it is not aligned to f0 — it rings and adds a second implied pitch instead of thickening.
  • Knob 4 reaches far enough that the whole window becomes a single cluster.
  • Density near 1/4, 1/2 and 1 lands on spectra that imply a subharmonic (with m = 2, d = 0.5 you get 1, 1.5, 3, 3.5 … = multiples of f0/2).
Cluster is the loud mode, and it is compensated for you — see Levels & gain compensation. If you build a version with the compensation defeated (NO_NORM=1), the density knob swings the level by many dB.

SHEPARD

Every partial in the window shifts toward the next one up: rk = k + φ. At φ = 1 the whole ensemble is identical to φ = 0 with the partials relabelled — so a continuously rising φ produces a glide that never restarts. Partial 1 travels a whole octave per wrap and partial 72 moves 24 cents, so the low partials sweep enormously and the high ones barely move; what earns the name is the wrap, not uniform motion.

  • Knob 4 = rate, exponential from 0.01 to 10 partials/second, centre frozen, CW rising. The illusion holds to roughly half a wrap per second — the usable range is the bottom of the knob. The top of the travel is a zap effect, deliberately.
  • The CLUSTERS jack is the better driver. Patch a slow ramp LFO into it. The firmware reads the jack's change, not its level, and folds any reset larger than half a wrap back into continuous motion — so the LFO's reset is not a click, and its depth does not have to be tuned to exactly one partial.
  • The ends fade. The bank is finite, so a shift leaves nothing at the bottom and pushes one off the top. Both ends fade to silence over four partials, keyed to the window rather than the bank, which is what makes the wrap exact. The cost is a narrow notch just above the window's bottom edge — static, a gap rather than a loop.
  • ODD ONLY doubles the cycle length: the odd set only lands back on itself after two index steps, so a wrap takes twice as long at the same apparent glide speed.
  • Knob 5 ducks the window, inverted so the knob at rest (CCW) is unity gain. Sweeping it up fades out just the moving partials and leaves the anchored ones playing.

10 · STEREO

The STEREO pot is not a width fader. It morphs between three stereo configurations and then spends its last quarter on motion rather than shape. The partials are distributed across eight pan slots by a fixed pattern that throws neighbouring partials as far apart as possible, so what you hear is a cloud rather than eight discrete positions.

What each stage sounds like

PotBehaviour
0 – 8%a genuine mono park. Nothing is panned; use it when the patch has to fold down.
8 – 50%morph from mono into SCATTER — a static, even fan of 8 positions. Neighbouring partials sit far apart, so a dense band smears into a wide sheet.
50 – 75%morph from SCATTER into ORBIT, with rotation creeping up from a standstill. Around the middle of this range you get a slow, barely-moving image.
75 – 100%the shape is fixed; the travel now buys motion. Rotation goes 0.04 → 0.20 revolutions/second, and the odd slot subset counter-rotates so the two clouds cross about twice per revolution.

Three things worth knowing

  • The fundamental is always centred. Spread is scaled by ratio − 1, so partial 1 sits dead centre and the image opens up above it. A fully spread fundamental smears the whole thing; anchoring it at the bottom is what makes wide settings still read as one note.
  • Nothing reaches a hard pan except SCATTER's two end slots. ORBIT's radius stops at 0.8 on purpose: hard panning puts half the bank in one channel at full gain instead of all of it at 0.707, which is the only thing here that ever moved a peak.
  • ODD ONLY does not shove the image sideways. Each configuration is built so the odd and even partial subsets stay balanced around centre — that property is asserted by a test, not hoped for.
Panel note: OUT LEFT and OUT RIGHT are correct as printed. Fox Tail is stereo out only — the two IN jacks are unused by this firmware (reading the audio input is against the module's noise budget).

11 · Levels and gain compensation

Fox Tail runs a fixed headroom budget — the Hammond drawbar answer — rather than dynamic normalisation. Nothing ducks when you push a slider up. Three fixed pieces of gain staging keep that honest:

1 · Headroom

A constant 0.30 sits in every band gain, sized against the worst case of all eight sliders at maximum. Because the partials start at random phases, a bank sums as √N — so an ordinary two- or three-band patch sits 6–9 dB below the case being protected. All sliders up lands around −19 dBFS RMS on the DARK slope. Fox Tail is a quiet module by design; expect to gain it up downstream.

2 · Tilt compensation

Summed over the bank, equal power per octave is about 5 dB louder than a sawtooth slope. The BRIGHT/DARK switch is level-compensated so throwing it changes the colour and not the loudness (worst error 0.06 dB).

3 · Cluster compensation

This is the big one. In CLUSTER the tilt and the band envelope are read at the shifted frequency, so collapsing the bank down-spectrum genuinely adds power — about 13 dB at full density. An attenuation derived from the geometry alone (density, cluster size, window) is folded into the band gains, holding RMS flat within 0.9 dB right across the density knob.

  • It only ever attenuates. Collapsing up (knob 4 CCW) actually gets quieter, and the model would ask for gain there — that request is refused, because it would also reach partials the window never moved.
  • It is computed from the control positions alone, never from the audio. That is not a style choice: gains derived from the engine's own amplitude state produced audible ~8 kHz artifacts on hardware, twice.
  • So: collapse down = compensated flat; collapse up = up to 0.4 dB quieter. Exactly 0.00 dB at the centre detent.

4 · The output clip

Linear below 0.85, parabolic to a hard cap at 1.15. As shipped nothing reaches the knee — an oscillator patch passes through bit-exact — so the clip is insurance for Cluster's beat transients, not a stage in the sound. Worst case measured over 22,875 patch combinations: peak 0.594, i.e. 3.1 dB of margin.

12 · LEDs

  • LED 1 follows INHARM.
  • LEDs 2–9 normally show band level. A band whose slider is up but whose partials have all passed Nyquist pulses dimly — “turned down” and “nothing left to play” must not look alike.
  • Touch WIN START or WIN SIZE and the row flips to a window peek — the lit LEDs show where the shaper window sits over the bank, with its edges partly lit — until 1.5 s after you stop moving the knob.

Calibration

Three steps, one stored record. Power up with INHARM fully up + left switch up + right switch down, then flip the right switch to capture each step:

  1. 1 V patched to V/OCT
  2. 3 V patched to V/OCT
  3. everything unpatched — this captures the CV jack nulls

Step 3 matters as much as the pitch fit: the summing jacks idle around −0.02 rather than 0, and since the firmware adds jack to knob and clamps, that bias ate the top 2.5% of every affected control's travel — which is why an uncalibrated unit can't quite reach a single cluster at full CW.

Bench-testing gotcha: on USB power alone the Daisy's analog domain is dead — CV inputs rail at ~0.99 and audio is silent, while the pots and sliders keep working normally. Always test CV and audio with rack power connected (USB may stay attached for the serial log).

Limits

  • 96 partials, always running; f0 clamped to 8 Hz – 6 kHz.
  • At 48 kHz, partials above Nyquist fade out over the top 5% of the range.
  • Phases are randomised at power-up and never reset — the module has no “retrigger”.

14 · Patch recipes

The endless glissando

  • Left switch up (SHEPARD)
  • WIN START fully CCW — window starts at partial 1
  • WIN SIZE fully CW — window covers the whole bank
  • WIN DUCK fully CCW — unity gain
  • All band sliders up. A drawn hump does not work here: the sliders control fill as well as level, so pulling the top band down thins it to one partial instead of fading it. The engine fades the ends for you.
  • Ramp LFO into the CLUSTERS jack, knob near centre, moderate depth, a few seconds per cycle.

The LFO is the illusion — without it there is only a static shifted spectrum. Either tilt works; ODD ONLY doubles the cycle length.

Skeleton comb

All band sliders at half: exactly eight partials, one per band, their positions chosen by the six FADE TILT pots (CCW = the band's lowest partial, centre = its middle, CW = its highest). Then sweep SHIFT to scan the whole shape up and down the harmonic series without retuning anything.

Metallic body, stable pitch

Left switch down (CLUSTER). Set WIN START above the fundamental so partials 1–3 stay anchored; WIN SIZE wide. Knob 4 a little CW of centre (small clusters), knob 5 up into the 0.9+ zone. Add INHARM to taste. The anchored low partials hold the note while everything above beats and rings.
